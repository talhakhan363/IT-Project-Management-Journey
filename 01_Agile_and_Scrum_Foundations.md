# Day 1: Agile, Scrum, and Waterfall Methodologies

## The Traditional Approach: Waterfall
The Waterfall model is a traditional, linear, and sequential approach to software development[cite: 4]. Progress flows in exactly one direction—downwards, and a new phase cannot start until the previous one is 100% complete and signed off.
* **Phases:** It typically consists of Requirements, Design, Implementation (Coding), Testing (QA), Deployment, and Maintenance.
* **The Flaw:** If building a mobile application, waiting months until the Testing phase to find out a specific feature drains the battery makes redesigning the system architecture incredibly expensive and time-consuming.

## The Modern Approach: Agile & Scrum
Agile is a philosophy, and Scrum is a specific framework used to put that philosophy into practice[cite: 27]. Agile is a mindset shift focused on speed, adaptability, and continuous delivery.
* **Sprints:** A short, fixed timeframe (usually 1 to 4 weeks) where a team commits to building a specific set of features.
* **Meetings:** The framework utilizes Sprint Planning, Daily Standups (quick 15-minute daily meetings), and Sprint Retrospectives to discuss what went well and what went wrong.

## Scrum Roles
A true Scrum team has three distinct roles, ensuring a balance of power and focus[cite: 87]:
* **Product Owner:** They own the vision of the product, prioritize the feature list, and define exactly what needs to be built.
* **Scrum Master:** They are the process expert who manages the Scrum process and removes any roadblocks or distractions preventing the team from working.
* **Development Team:** The engineers, designers, and QA testers who actually build the product and decide how to technically achieve the goals.

## Visualizing Work: Kanban
Kanban is another Agile framework, often used alongside Scrum, with the primary goal to visualize work and maximize efficiency.
* **The Board:** Work is represented by cards that move across columns, typically: Backlog ➔ To Do ➔ In Progress ➔ In Review ➔ Done.
* **WIP Limits (Work In Progress):** Kanban heavily restricts how many tasks can be in the "In Progress" column at one time. This forces teams to finish what they started before starting something new.
